## What's ranking now, and what it misses

Live sweep 2026-08-30:

1. **ChAD** — the Chambre de l'assurance de dommages, the regulator that
   governs claims adjusters. Written *for adjusters*, about their own duties.
2. **Wawanesa** — insurer, Quebec-specific water claim guide.
3. **iA Groupe financier** — insurer, a five-step aide-mémoire.
4. **quebecassurances.org** — affiliate/lead site promising payment in 21 days.

Every one of these is written from the insurer's side of the table, or in
ChAD's case from the adjuster's. The homeowner's own documents — the photos,
the readings, the scope — are treated as inputs that arrive from somewhere.

**The gap we're filling:** nobody writes this from the chair where the
documentation is actually produced. We are the ones taking the moisture
readings and writing the scope the adjuster works from, so we can say which
documents move a file, which ones stall it, and what a homeowner can do while
waiting rather than just waiting.

## Our angle

Two things we can say that no insurer will put on its own site.

**The wait is not always required.** ChAD's guidance to its own members says the
adjuster is responsible for coordinating urgent measures, and that failing to
implement them in time — opening walls and ceilings, ensuring ventilation —
breaches professional duties and can let mould proliferate, dramatically raising
costs. A homeowner told to sit tight until someone visits is entitled to know
that the adjuster's own regulator treats delay as a problem, not as procedure.

**Mitigation is the homeowner's obligation, not an overstep.** The insured must
limit damage and preserve evidence. Drying early is not "starting work before
approval" — it is the thing the policy expects.

That reframes the whole waiting period, and it is the written form of what
`/services/water-damage` already claims about readings and written scope.

## Blockers

**Tone check, and it is the whole risk of this post.** The ChAD material makes
it possible to write something that reads as "your adjuster is failing you and
here is the article number to quote at them." That would be a different post,
it would be worse, and it would put you in the middle of arguments you don't
want. The draft reports what the regulator asks of adjusters so a homeowner
understands the process, and keeps the practical advice on their own side of
the table — mitigate, document, ask. Confirm that's the line you want before it
lands.
