## The query

**Secondary:** « qui est responsable dégât d'eau copropriété » · « franchise

**Intent:** regulatory, under pressure — the loss has already happened
**Who types it:** a unit owner whose ceiling is stained, a board member facing a
deductible, or a tenant being told it's their problem.

## What's ranking now, and what it misses

Live sweep 2026-08-30:

1. **iA Groupe financier** — insurer. Explains the two-policy structure well.
2. **Radio-Canada** — reporting on deductibles reaching tens of thousands, and
   up to half a million in some downtown Montreal towers.
3. **Sonnet** — insurer. "Qui paie quoi" in general terms.
4. **Dunton Rainville** — law firm, and the most legally precise of the set.
5. **Soumissions Copropriété / hoodi.ai** — lead sites.

**The gap we're filling:** every result on this SERP explains who is *liable*.
None of them is the contractor who then has to repair the unit, so none of them
covers what actually happens next — which insurer's adjuster you are waiting on,
what documentation each one needs, and why the repair stalls in the gap between
the syndicate's file and yours.

## Our angle

We are not an insurer or a lawyer, and the post should not pretend otherwise.
What we have that none of the five results has: we are in the unit afterwards.
We can report the legal framework accurately with attribution, then answer the
question the lawyers and insurers stop short of — *why is nobody starting the
repair, and what unsticks it.*

The counterintuitive fact is the spine of the post: **liability is not presumed
even when everyone knows where the water came from.** Most readers arrive
certain that the unit above pays. The Civil Code says otherwise, and that single
correction is worth the whole page.

reports the legal framework with named sources and does not advise. It never

declaration and their own insurer. If you want it softer than that, say so
before it lands — it is easier to cut than to un-publish.
