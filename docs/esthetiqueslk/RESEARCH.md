## 4. Reviews and differentiators

- Fresha: **5.0/5 from 8 reviews** on the captured listing. This is a small sample and should be labelled as Fresha reviews, not presented as a universal rating.
- Review themes: Bianka praised Marie as caring/professional and the quality, products and delicacy of the facial; Isma described Selma as gentle and professional; older reviews mention facial classique + microdermabrasion + radiofréquence and facial de luxe, with satisfaction and recommendations.
- CanadaNear has one public review praising the result of a **protein treatment** after the first wash; the directory’s NAP/hours conflict with other sources, so use the review only as a qualitative lead.
- Differentiators supportable from public sources: personalized/needs-adapted care; facial and body results content; Beauty Pot 4-in-1 body-contouring technology; oxygen/radiofrequency facial; Japanese head spa; Fresha instant booking; a visible education/training angle; French-first local Laval positioning.
- “Medical-grade,” “expert,” “safe,” “no danger,” guaranteed results, or permanent fat loss should not be repeated as site claims without credentials, device details, evidence and regulatory review.

## 5. Language findings

- Public Fresha has French and English URL renderings, but the business description, service names, reviews and Instagram content are overwhelmingly **French**.
- The public evidence does **not** prove that staff offer full service in English. Build a French-first site with an EN toggle only after SLK confirms who can serve English-speaking clients and which pages should be translated.
- Avoid machine-translating clinical/aftercare copy; have the practitioner review both languages.

## 7. Recommended site IA

1. **Accueil** — French-first promise, Laval location, “Réserver” and “Consultation” CTAs, three proof points (personalized care, visible results, Fresha booking).
2. **Soins du visage** — facial classique/deep facial, microdermabrasion, oxygen + radiofrequency, anti-ageing only if confirmed, chemical peel with candid downtime/aftercare.
3. **Remodelage corporel** — Beauty Pot modalities, goals/limitations, consultation-first CTA, before/after consent note.
4. **Épilation & regard** — waxing, laser hair removal, brows, lash extensions/semi-permanent makeup only as confirmed.
5. **Head spa, corps & bien-être** — Japanese head spa, foot spa, massage and other body care; publish only live services.
6. **Soins capillaires** — silk-protein smoothing and confirmed hair services.
7. **Résultats & avis** — consented before/after gallery, Fresha review excerpts, date/service context and realistic-results disclaimer.
8. **À propos / l’équipe** — founder story, credentials, current team, approach and hygiene standards (facts supplied by SLK).
9. **Tarifs & réservation** — live Fresha button/deep links, duration/price cards, deposit/cancellation policy, no-walk-in note.
10. **Contact / FAQ** — confirmed NAP, hours, map, parking/access, languages, contraindications/prep/aftercare and privacy/consent.
11. **EN version** — implement after English-service capability and translations are confirmed.

Use Service schema only for services that have a confirmed name, price/duration and current booking URL. Use LocalBusiness schema after NAP is reconciled.

## 8. Photo / Instagram pull shot list

Use the public account as a visual reference and request permission/original files before republishing client imagery. The profile’s highlights are useful buckets: **Avis clientes, Résultats, Peau, Corps, Produits, Prix.** Prioritize:

1. Exterior/building and suite 206 entrance with exact address signage.
2. Bright, clean treatment-room wide shot; bed, device and hygiene setup.
3. Practitioner portrait(s) and action portrait(s), with names/credentials approved.
4. Facial treatment in progress: gloved hands, cleansing/extraction/microdermabrasion and calm client experience.
5. Oxygen/radiofrequency facial device and close-up, with model/device name verified.
6. Chemical-peel application and a non-graphic, consented skin-results sequence; include aftercare products and timeline.
7. Body-contouring action shot showing the Beauty Pot handpiece and a wide, non-identifying client framing.
8. Consent-cleared body before/after pairs, labelled by treatment and number of sessions; no misleading crop/lighting.
9. Product shelf/aftercare still life with readable product names and brand permission.
10. Japanese head spa, foot spa and massage atmospherics if these are current services.
11. Brows/lashes/semi-permanent work close-ups, if currently offered and model consent is documented.
12. Consultation scene: practitioner listening, skin/body assessment and a clear “consultation” CTA.
13. Team/group photo and warm Laval-local identity image.
14. Simple price/menu graphics rebuilt from confirmed Fresha data rather than screenshots whose prices may age.

For launch, use 6–10 strongest images: one room/people image, one facial action image, one peel result, one body result, one device/technology image, one product/aftercare image, one team/consultation image and one review/brand graphic. Keep the original Instagram handle credited where appropriate; obtain written release for recognizable clients and do not imply a medical outcome.
