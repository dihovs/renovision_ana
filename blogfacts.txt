[law] IN FORCE — WHAT THE OWNER MUST DO ON A DANGEROUS CONDITION (FACADES). Art. 376 imposes six sequential duties when a dangerous condition is detected 'en cours de vérification ou autrement': 1° 'mettre en place sans délai les mesures d'urgence pour assurer la sécurité des occupants et du public'; 2° 'en aviser la Régie sans délai'; 3° 'fournir par écrit à la Régie, DANS LES 30 JOURS, une description, élaborée par un ingénieur ou un architecte, des travaux correctifs à réaliser pour éliminer la condition dangereuse de même que, POUR APPROBATION, un échéancier des travaux correctifs'; 4° ensure the work is done per that description, plan and schedule; 5° obtain, at completion, a verification report confirming the facades are safe; 6° 'transmettre à la Régie une lettre signée par l'ingénieur ou l'architecte confirmant que tous les travaux correctifs sont complétés à sa satisfaction et qu'il n'y a plus de condition dangereuse.' [law] IN FORCE — 'DEFECT' IS NOT 'DANGEROUS CONDITION'; THE DUTIES DIFFER. [advice] WHO BEARS THE COST — the recourse against the seller. Both located cases were decided under the Civil Code latent-defect regime rather than under environmental orders: soil contamination from a buried oil tank was held to be a «vice caché» engaging the seller's legal warranty of quality (art. 1726 C.c.Q.), with the remedy being a reduction of the sale price (art. 1728 / art. 1604 C.c.Q.). [law] CORRECTION OF A WIDELY REPEATED FALSE CLAIM. Numerous contractor and insurance-broker blogs state that Quebec law requires replacement of residential heating-oil TANKS older than 20 years. That is not what the regulation says. The Règlement sur les appareils de chauffage au mazout, art. 7, bans REPAIRING an oil-fired «chaudière, générateur d'air chaud ou chauffe-eau» in an existing residential building from 31 December 2023 where the APPLIANCE was installed more than 20 years earlier (10 years for a water heater) — and separately bans replacing the combustion chamber or heat exchanger outright. Q-2, r. 1.1, art. 2 defines «bâtiment résidentiel» as a building meeting ALL of: (1) «l'aire du bâtiment est d'au plus 600 m2»; (2) «la hauteur du bâtiment est d'au plus 3 étages»; (3) «l'usage principal du bâtiment est du groupe C - Habitation et il n'abrite que des logements», qualified per the Code national du bâtiment – Canada 2015. «Bâtiment résidentiel existant» = construction permit issued before 31 December 2021; «neuf» = on or after that date. Larger or mixed-use residential buildings fall outside this regulation (though LQE art. 21 still applies to them).

[law] TANK-REMOVAL PAPER TRAIL. Q-2, r. 1.1, art. 8 requires the person who installs an oil-fired (or replacement fossil-fuel) appliance in a residential building to file an electronic declaration with the Minister within 30 working days, which must include, at para. 4: «une description de la procédure suivie lors de l'enlèvement du réservoir qui alimentait en combustible l'appareil remplacé, le cas échéant.» Art. 9 imposes a parallel 30-working-day declaration when an oil appliance is replaced by one running on a different form of energy. So tank removal generates a government record — relevant both to homeowners and to contractors performing the work.

[applicability] RBQ PETROLEUM-EQUIPMENT REGIME — thresholds matter, and most houses fall below them. RBQ classifies petroleum equipment by risk; per RBQ's «Équipements pétroliers visés» page, underground residential heating-oil tanks are high-risk at 4,000 litres and more, and above-ground tanks at 10,000 litres and more, and it states «Les équipements de moins de 10 000 litres d'une maison unifamiliale ne sont pas considérés comme des équipements pétroliers à risque élevé.» A typical single-family tank (roughly 900–2,300 L) is therefore not high-risk petroleum equipment, and the permit/inventory/periodic-verification duties RBQ imposes on owners and operators are not the framework that governs an ordinary house. RBQ's own owner-obligations page cites Code de sécurité chapter VI and RLRQ B-1.1, r. 9. CAUTION: I could not open the underlying Code de construction ch. VIII / Code de sécurité ch. VI provisions to confirm chapter numbering and the exact litre thresholds against the regulation text; verify before asserting these numbers in print.

[advice] CONTEXT ON WHY LEAKS ARE ABOUT TO SURFACE — useful framing, officially sourced. Québec.ca's residential oil-heating page notes that leaks or spills from residential oil heating equipment contaminate soil and can affect groundwater and surface water, and that ageing tanks carry a risk of hydrocarbon leaks. Combined with the 31 December 2023 conversion trigger and MELCCFP's own expectation of «la découverte potentielle de nombreux terrains résidentiels contaminés», the credible editorial angle is that tank decommissioning during conversion is the moment contamination is typically discovered — which is exactly when a homeowner needs to know the art. 21 duty and their insurance position.
